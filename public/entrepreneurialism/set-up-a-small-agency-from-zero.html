How I'd set up a small agency from zero

By Waleed Khan · 13 November 2026

A small agency needs very little money to start. It needs a clear offer, a few proofs that you can deliver, and simple habits that keep clients happy. This is the order I would follow if I were starting again. I started by offering graphic design services, and I got very good at it.

1. Pick one service

"We do everything" tells a client nothing. Choose one service you can deliver well and say it in one sentence. You can add more later. I started with graphic design, especially logo design. Then I moved on to branding, then social media, and then print. The important thing is to start with one skill and add related ones on top of it.

2. Choose who it is for

Decide on one kind of client: shops, clinics, restaurants, startups. A narrow group is easier to find, easier to talk to, and easier to impress with relevant examples. To succeed more easily, focus on one niche, for example beauty salons or barbershops. It could be anything. Once you have a pitch that works for one, you can pitch the next customer with it, because they are all almost the same, and you build experience with that kind of client.

3. Build proof before you sell

Start with your own website, then do two or three small projects, even at a lower price or for people you know. Ask each for a short testimonial and permission to show the work. Two real examples beat a long list of promises.

4. Price in packages

Offer two or three fixed packages with a clear list of what is included and what costs extra. Take a deposit before starting, for example half upfront and half on delivery. Fixed scopes stop "one more small change" from eating your time.

5. Keep the tools simple

You need only a handful: email on your own domain, a way to send invoices and track money, a list of clients and leads, a contract template, and one place to track each project. For the agency I use Zoho Mail, Zoho Books and Zoho CRM. Add more only when something breaks.

6. Find your first clients

Start with people who already know you, then nearby businesses, then referrals from happy clients. Partner programmes can add income without extra work, such as earning a commission for referring a service you already trust. My own clients started with friends, and then friends of friends. For young people just starting out, the way to land your first clients is to show your skills and experience, and the easiest way to do that is to offer your work free or at a discount at the start.

7. Run every project the same way

A short brief, a written scope, clear milestones, a limited number of revision rounds, and a final handover. When every project follows the same steps, quality stays steady and clients know what to expect.

8. Handle the legal side early

Register the business properly, use a written agreement for every job, and keep your money and records separate from personal ones. One thing I want to stress is to be clear with clients. Everything should be legal and clearly written down: what you are offering and what you are not.

9. Earn from clients after the project ends

Websites need hosting, updates and care. Offering these as a monthly service gives you steadier income than one-off projects and keeps you in touch with the client.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying yes to every kind of work.
  • Starting without a deposit or a written scope.
  • Copying big agencies' pricing and processes before you have the clients to support them.
  • Forgetting to ask for reviews while the client is happy.

Where I would start

If I were starting again, in the first week I would pick one skill and one kind of client, make a few pieces of work to show, and offer them free or at a discount to people I know. I would write down clearly what I offer and what I do not. Everything else can be added later.
