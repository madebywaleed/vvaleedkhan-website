Entrepreneurialism

What running more than one business taught me

By Waleed Khan · 13 November 2026

Running one business is hard. Running several changes what the job is. For me it all comes down to practice, experience and an inner instinct to keep going and keep improving.

1. You stop being the person who does the work

With one business you can do everything yourself. With several you cannot be in every one of them, so your job becomes setting direction, choosing people and checking results. For most of my life I have been a one-man show, but running multiple businesses makes that impossible: you have to hire people. You will not hire the right people on the first try. You will make mistakes, and it takes time to learn who is good, who is best, and what kind of person or talent works best for which task or business.

2. Attention is the real budget, not money

Money can be borrowed or earned back. A day cannot. Every new venture takes attention from the others, so the question is not "can I afford it?" but "what will get less of me if I do this?" Time is a rare resource: you have only one mind and a few hours a day, and you cannot give them to every business. A fruit and vegetable business I ran alongside my agency needed me there in person, which left little attention for anything else. The business that gets most of your time will be the most polished and the one most likely to succeed.

3. Each business needs its own numbers

When the money of several businesses blends together, a weak one hides behind a strong one. Keep separate accounts, separate reports and a separate yes-or-no on whether each one is earning its place. Never run a business on another business's money. Let each one stand on its own. Pay yourself a salary from the business, and do not take all the profit out as pocket money.

4. Something is always neglected, so choose it on purpose

You cannot give equal time to everything. Decide which business gets your best hours this quarter, and accept that another will run on the minimum. Even when you manage several businesses, you have to run on priorities. Without them you end up with one successful business and the others left behind.

5. Skills and contacts carry from one business to the next

Suppliers, customers, tools and lessons from one venture often help another. For me, the most helpful has been the marketing agency. It helped my other businesses start and thrive, because the foundation of any business is a conversation with marketers.

6. Knowing when to stop is a skill

Starting is exciting and stopping is not, but a business that drains time and gives nothing back costs more than it shows. If something is not working for the time being, it is important to stop. I stopped my printing business because it was taking too much time and needed more capital to earn good revenue. If I had kept running it, it would still have survived, but the revenue it generated was not enough and it was slowing me down. The opposite mistake is just as real. I closed several early businesses before they had a fair test, sometimes before six months or a year, and that was my biggest mistake. Stop when a business has had a fair chance and still does not work. I plan to start a printing business again, with what I have learned since.

Final thought

One of the most important things I have learned from running multiple businesses is that skills transfer. The more businesses you run, the better you get at it, until there comes a time when starting and managing a business becomes easy. It is not always about the money. It is about skills and experience too.
