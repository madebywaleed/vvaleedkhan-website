Entrepreneurialism

Lessons from 16 years in business

By Waleed Khan · 13 November 2026

I started in business in 2010, teaching people what I was good at at the time. I started by instructing IELTS, and then I started an academy to teach English conversation.

Where it started and how it changed

It started with an English academy. I rented a room in a school and did some advertising among friends. My very first customers, the students, were my own friends. That is when I learned that teaching was only a temporary skill for me. My main talent is managing businesses: an innate feeling for how to do things better. The academy also taught me how important the basic skills of management are. I wound up most of my early businesses because of a busy schedule while I was doing my master's, and they were suffering for it.

Lesson 1: Cash matters more than profit on paper

A business can look profitable and still run out of money, because customers pay late and bills arrive early. Watch what is in the bank, not only what the books say. It is very important to keep cash, or readily available money. Expenses and bills should not come before revenue. When you make a profit, then decide which services to sign up for or what to improve. And for most things, never pay in advance: pay afterwards, if the product or service allows it.

Lesson 2: Choose people slowly, and let go of the wrong ones early

The wrong hire or partner costs more than an empty chair. Over the years I have worked with many people as partners. You never know who is worth keeping and who is worth letting go. Time decides, and time teaches. You cannot judge someone by the cover, and you will find some extraordinary people hiding among simple, straightforward employees.

Lesson 3: Say no to work that does not fit

Not every customer or opportunity is worth taking. Some cost more in time and stress than they pay. As you grow, you will meet friends who are also clients and do not pay you. They waste your time and resources, so keep away from them early.

Lesson 4: Reputation is slow to build and quick to lose

In most trades, repeat customers and word of mouth matter more than advertising. I always focused on giving extra at the start and never compromised on quality. Quality work brings customers from far and wide. Low-quality work kills your customers as early as the first product or service you sell them.

Lesson 5: Keep records from day one

Invoices, agreements and accounts feel boring until you need them. Business success depends on how much data you have. Never do blind business, not knowing how much you invested, earned, lost or owe. Always keep a detailed record of your business.

Lesson 6: Start small and test before you commit

You learn more from a small first version than from months of planning. One of the most common things I hear from people is that you need a lot of money to start. They think that with plenty of money you can open a bigger store or business and quickly become successful. That is wrong. You have to do one of two things: test the market by doing business, or do such thorough research that you believe the business will succeed whatever the cost.

Lesson 7: Keep learning the tools

Technology changes how every business works, from printing to apps to websites. Be an early adopter to stay ahead in the game. Test the new tools that could work for you and use them. They will help you and increase your efficiency in the long run.

Lesson 8: Patience

Most businesses take longer to work than the owner expects. I feel you need a minimum of one year to test a business: to see whether it will work and when it starts to generate revenue.

What I would tell myself in 2010

Stick to one business that you can do well, even if it is small or you are short on capital. For example, I started a fruit and vegetable business twice. If I had stuck to it, maybe it would have become a superstore for fruit and vegetables. Stay focused, work smart, and keep going.

Looking ahead

After sixteen years, my advice is the same as what I would tell myself in 2010: stay focused, work smart and keep going. I plan to start a printing business again, this time with better records, more patience and a clearer focus. Skills and experience build up with every business, and that is worth more than money.
